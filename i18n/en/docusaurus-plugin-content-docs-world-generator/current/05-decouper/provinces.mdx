# Provinces

Divides the map into the smallest unit a strategy game plays on. Everything in the [Hierarchy](./hierarchie) panel is built on them.

**Primary action** — *Generate provinces*, in the pinned bar. The brush button beside it jumps to hand editing.

## How it works

Provinces are seeded across the map and grow until they meet, in **two independent populations** — land and sea — so an ocean is not chopped into the same fine mesh as a continent.

Seeding density is modulated twice: once per **elevation stratum**, again per **biome**. That is how you get finely cut mountains and coarse plains out of a single pass.

## Counts

*Open by default.*

| Read-out | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Land provinces** | How many exist right now |
| **Sea provinces** | Likewise for water |

| Control | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Land target** | 50 – 50,000 | Roughly how many land provinces to aim for |
| **Sea target** | 10 – 50,000 | Likewise for water |

These are targets, not guarantees. The actual count depends on how much land there is and on the density modifiers below.

:::tip Start small
800 land provinces generate in seconds and tell you whether the shape is right. Move to production numbers once you are happy.
:::

## Irregularity

*Collapsed by default.*

| Control | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Land chaos** | 0 – 10 | Irregularity of land boundaries |
| **Sea chaos** | 0 – 10 | Likewise for water |

At 0 the result is close to a clean Voronoi tessellation — provinces read as geometric cells. Raising it perturbs the boundaries into something that looks drawn rather than computed.

## Cleanup

*Collapsed by default. Requires provinces.*

Generation leaves slivers: one-pixel provinces wedged between larger ones, mostly along coastlines.

| Control | Range |
|---|---|
| **Minimum size in pixels** | 1 – 100 |

**Clean micro-provinces** absorbs everything below that threshold into its neighbours and relinks the hierarchy afterwards, so no territory points at a province that has gone.

The notification reports how many were removed.

## Density per stratum

*Collapsed by default.*

One row per elevation band, each showing in brackets how many provinces it holds.

| Control | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Density** | 0.01 – 3 | Seeding-density multiplier for that band |

Below 1, larger and sparser provinces. Above, smaller and denser.

Each row also carries a **Regenerate** button that re-runs generation **for that band only**, leaving the rest of the map alone. That is what makes tuning practical: adjust the mountains without waiting for the oceans.

The button reads *Generate first* and stays disabled until provinces exist.

## Biome density

*Collapsed by default.*

A second multiplier, applied on top of the stratum density, one row per enabled biome.

| Control | Range |
|---|---|
| **Modifier** | 0.1 – 5 |

1.0 leaves the stratum density alone. Use it when the distinction you want is ecological rather than topographic — dense provinces through farmland, sparse through desert, though both sit in the same elevation band.

## Hand editing

The brush button in the action bar takes you to [Sculpt → Province editing](../sculpter/sculpter#province-editing), edit mode already on.

Remember to press **Apply new province** on the way out: painting changes the pixel map immediately, but adjacency and the hierarchy are only rebuilt on request.

## What gets exported

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `provinces.png` | Colour map, one distinct colour per province |
| `definition.csv` | ID ↔ RGB, contiguous IDs from 1 |
| `adjacencies.csv` | Which provinces share a border |
| `provinces.csv` | Per-province data, via *Export → Individual files* |

IDs are **renumbered contiguously from 1** at export time, so gaps left by deletion or hand editing never reach the engine.
