# Preparing your first world

<Sheet>

**Goal** — Walk the whole pipeline once, at a small resolution, to see every stage before committing to a full-size map.

**Duration** — 10 minutes.

**Prerequisites** — [Installation](../overview/installation): the application starts and the map is drawn.

</Sheet>

## Part 1 — Find a world

The application opens on the **Terrain** panel, with a world already generated.

<Capture
  src="/img/wgen/12-barre-actions.png"
  caption="The action bar, pinned at the bottom of the panel"
  fig="Fig. 3.1"
/>

Press the **dice** button, to the right of **Generate map**, to roll a new seed and regenerate. Keep going until a coastline appeals to you.

:::tip The bar does not scroll
It stays put wherever you are in the panel. You can change a setting at the very bottom and apply it without scrolling back up.
:::

### Keep the seed

Open the **Seed** group, type a name into *Name this world*, then **Keep**.

<Capture
  src="/img/wgen/11-graine.png"
  caption="The Seed group and the saved-seed shelf"
  fig="Fig. 3.2"
/>

The seed goes onto the shelf and into `seeds.txt`. You will be able to come back to exactly this world later.

## Part 2 — Shape the terrain

Open the **Noise** group and try these, pressing **Generate map** after each change:

| Setting | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Scale** | Low = few large landmasses; high = archipelago |
| **Octaves** | Detail. 3 is smooth and rounded, 8 is craggy |
| **Sea level** | Raise it to drown the map, lower it to expose the seabed |
| **Edge mask** | Fades the map toward its borders, so continents do not run off the frame |

Then open **Shaping** and switch on **Warped coastlines**. Fractal noise alone makes rounded blobs; displacing it before sampling gives inlets, peninsulas and fjords.

## Part 3 — Choose the resolution

Open the **Resolution** group. For this walkthrough stay on **1080p**: generation takes seconds instead of minutes, and every stage below behaves identically.

The note under the presets says what that size costs in memory. Come back to **Archway** (8129 × 4065) once you know the settings you want.

## Part 4 — Paint by hand

Switch to the **Sculpt** tab, the brush icon.

<Capture
  src="/img/wgen/20-panneau-sculpt.png"
  caption="The Sculpt panel in topographic view"
  fig="Fig. 3.3"
  zoom
/>

The view switcher at the top of the panel decides what the map shows. In **Topo** view the brush paints elevation bands directly:

1. Open **Paint** and choose a target stratum — *Mountainous*, say.
2. Open **Brush** and set a **Size** you can see on screen.
3. Click and drag on the map.

Useful while painting, without leaving the canvas:

| Shortcut | Effect |
|---|---|
| `Ctrl` + scroll | Brush size |
| `Alt` + scroll | Falloff — up for softer |
| `Ctrl`+`Alt` + scroll | Rotation |
| Scroll | Zoom |
| Middle-drag | Pan |
| `Ctrl`+`Z` | Undo |

:::tip Regenerating does not erase this
Painted influence is kept when the underlying noise is regenerated. You can go back to Terrain, change the seed, and your mountains stay put.
:::

## Part 5 — Cut into provinces

Switch to the **Provinces** tab.

<Capture
  src="/img/wgen/30-panneau-provinces.png"
  caption="The Provinces panel"
  fig="Fig. 3.4"
  zoom
/>

1. Open **Counts** and set **Land target** to something modest — 800 for a first run.
2. Press **Generate provinces** in the pinned bar.

The view switches to provinces automatically, each with its own colour.

If the result is too regular, open **Irregularity** and raise **Land chaos**. If mountains should be more finely cut than plains, open **Density per stratum** and raise the multiplier for the high bands.

## Part 6 — Group into territories

Switch to the **Hierarchy** tab.

1. Open **Build**, press **New territory level**.
2. Name it — *Counties* — and leave **Built from** on *Provinces*.
3. **Create level**. Grouping runs on its own.

Do it again for the next tier: create *Duchies*, but this time choose **Counties** as the source. Each level groups the one below.

**Min size** and **Max size** decide how many source units go into each territory. To assign one by hand: select a level, switch on **Paint mode** in the **Selected territory** group, left-click a territory to select it, then right-click the provinces to pour into it.

## Part 7 — Export

Switch to the **Export** tab and press **Export all maps** in the pinned bar.

Everything lands in an `Export/` folder next to the executable:

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `heightmap.png` | Elevation as 16-bit greyscale |
| `heightmap.raw` | The same data as 16-bit RAW |
| `normalmap.png` | Derived from the final heightmap |
| `provinces.png` | Province colour map |
| `definition.csv` | ID ↔ colour table |
| `adjacencies.csv` | Which provinces touch which |
| `Counties.csv`, `Duchies.csv`, … | One per hierarchy level |

Before exporting, the **Heightmap** group is worth a look. **Stratum slope** at 0 keeps hard terraces — best if you are going to edit the heightmap afterwards. Raising it bevels the boundaries between bands, which reads better dropped straight into an engine.

## Part 8 — Save

`Ctrl`+`S` writes the world into `saves/`. The document name at the top of the window stops saying *Untitled*.

A `.wgen` holds everything: terrain, painted influence, provinces, the full hierarchy, masks and stencil. Reopening it puts you exactly where you left off.
